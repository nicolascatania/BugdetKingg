1. About this service

BudgetKing (“the service”, “the app”) is a personal finance tracker built and operated by Nicolás Catania, an individual software developer based in Argentina (“I”, “me”). It is a personal and portfolio project, offered free of charge. It is not a company, not a bank, and not a financial institution of any kind.

The source code is publicly available on GitHub, so anything stated here can be checked against what the code actually does.

By signing in you accept these terms. If you do not agree with them, simply do not use the service.

2. Your account

Sign-in is through your Google account only. There is no BudgetKing password. Your account is created automatically the first time you sign in, and every later sign-in with the same Google account reuses it.

  • You must have a Google account with a verified email address.
  • You are responsible for keeping your Google account secure.
  • One person, one account. Do not use the service to hold data on behalf of other people without their consent.

3. What data we collect

3.1 From Google, when you sign in

Google hands the app a signed identity token. From it the following claims are read and stored:

  • Google subject id — a stable, opaque identifier for your Google account, used to recognise you on later sign-ins.
  • Email address — used as your unique account identifier and shown inside the app.
  • Given name and family name — shown inside the app.
  • Profile photo URL — a link to the picture Google serves; the image itself is not copied.

Nothing else. The app never sees your Google password, your contacts, your calendar, your Drive, your Gmail or any other Google data. The Google token is verified once and then discarded; it is not stored.

3.2 What you enter yourself

Everything you create in the app is stored so it can be shown back to you:

  • Accounts — name, description, balance and icon.
  • Categories — name and icon.
  • Transactions — date, amount, type, description, counterparty, account(s), category and any linked savings goal.
  • Recurring transactions — the same fields plus frequency, next run date, end date and active flag.
  • Budgets — category, month, year and spending limit.
  • Savings goals — name, icon, target amount and date, current amount, linked account and status.
  • CSV files you import — parsed into the records above; the file itself is not kept.

Each record also carries a creation and last-update timestamp and the email of the account that created or changed it (which is always your own).

3.3 Technical data

  • IP address — used in memory on the server to limit how many sign-in attempts a single address can make per minute. It is not written to the database.
  • Hosting provider logs — the platforms that host the app (see section 6) keep standard request logs (IP, time, path, user agent) for their own operational purposes and retention periods, outside my control.

There is no analytics, no tracking pixel, no advertising SDK and no fingerprinting of any kind in the app.

4. How we use it

Your data is used for exactly one thing: running the app for you.

  • Identifying you when you sign in and showing your own records back to you.
  • Computing balances, budget progress, goal progress and dashboard charts.
  • Enforcing security controls such as rate limiting.

Your data is never:

  • sold, rented or licensed to anyone;
  • shared with third parties for marketing or advertising;
  • used to build profiles, train models or make automated decisions about you;
  • used to contact you, unless you contacted me first.

As the operator I have administrator access to the database and to an administration screen that lists registered users (name, email, photo, roles). I use it only to keep the service running and to honour requests from you.

5. Where it is stored

  • The frontend (what runs in your browser) is served from GitHub Pages.
  • The backend runs on Render, on a free plan.
  • Records are kept in a MySQL database hosted by a third-party cloud database provider.

All traffic between your browser, the backend and Google travels over HTTPS. Access to the database is restricted to the backend and to me.

Your records are kept for as long as your account exists. See section 8 for how to have them deleted.

6. Third-party services

The app relies on these providers. Each has its own privacy policy that applies to what they receive.

  • Google Identity Services — renders the “Sign in with Google” button and issues the identity token. Google may set its own cookies when you use it. Google Privacy Policy.
  • Google Fonts — serves the typefaces; your browser requests them directly from Google.
  • Cloudflare cdnjs — serves the Font Awesome icon set; your browser requests it directly.
  • GitHub Pages and Render — hosting, as described in section 5.
  • dolarapi.com and argentinadatos.com — public sources for the official dollar rate and inflation figures shown on the dashboard. These are called by the backend, not by your browser, and receive no data about you.

7. Cookies and local storage

BudgetKing itself sets no cookies. It keeps two values in your browser's local storage:

  • jwt_token — your session token, valid for 24 hours, removed when you sign out.
  • budgetking-theme — your light/dark preference, if you chose one.

Clearing your browser's site data removes both. The Google sign-in button, being served by Google, may set Google's own cookies under Google's policy.

8. Your rights

Under Argentina's Personal Data Protection Act (Law 25.326) and similar laws elsewhere, you can:

  • Access your data — it is all visible in the app, and accounts, categories and transactions can be exported to CSV at any time.
  • Correct it — every record can be edited in the app. Your photo is refreshed from Google on every sign-in; your name is copied once, on your first sign-in — ask me (section 12) if it needs updating.
  • Delete it — contact me (section 12) and your account and every record attached to it will be permanently removed within 30 days. Deletion is not reversible.
  • Withdraw Google's permission — remove BudgetKing from the “Third-party apps” section of your Google account settings. Your records stay until you ask for deletion.

The Agencia de Acceso a la Información Pública (AAIP), as the enforcement authority for Law 25.326, handles complaints about non-compliance with personal data protection rules.
